Definitions and how to interpret the controls

C1 is the mean, C2 the variance, C3 the third cumulant, and C4 = fourth central moment − 3 variance². Estimates use finite-sample-corrected k-statistics. Ratios are formed after centrality-bin cumulants are averaged.

Proton factorial cumulants: F2=C2−C1, F3=C3−3C2+2C1, F4=C4−6C3+11C2−6C1. Normalized values divide by mean proton count to the corresponding power. These options apply to proton/antiproton counts, not to signed net-proton number.

Shared boost: each clump has one common direction. Event mixed: each source proton has an independent direction sampled from the same ensemble. With fixed T, beta and transverse directions, their full-azimuth count laws coincide; the paired count sampler deliberately couples these identical laws exactly. Isotropic 3D directions can produce different count fluctuations through a shared longitudinal boost. Poisson yield control: an ungated independent Poisson excess matched in expected accepted mean, removing the rare signal-event class mixture.

Phi scrambling preserves accepted counts exactly and therefore gives identical global count cumulants. It is not plotted as a separate curve here. Angular or differential observables are needed to distinguish it.

Uncertainties: paired bootstrap resampling of independent event blocks. The same block weights are used for background, signal and all scan points. Difference intervals account for their covariance. The pilot intervals are approximate and are checked against event-level bootstrap on representative points; small antiproton samples or rare signal classes can have broad/unstable fourth-order estimates. Multiple scan points are not independent discovery tests.

Analytic dashed curves: the measured background cumulants plus the exact source-count cumulants from the integrated-acceptance model, transformed into the selected statistic. They are closure predictions, not fits. Rapidity/pt cuts use full emitted parent distributions with no inherited |eta|<1 restriction.
